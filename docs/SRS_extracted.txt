1.  Introduction
1.1  Purpose of this Document
This Software Requirements Specification defines what the SIF Sentinel AI prototype does, how it is built, and how it will be demonstrated. It is written for three audiences at once: the SIH evaluation panel who need to judge feasibility and innovation, the domain reviewers from Oil India Limited who need to confirm that the safety logic is credible, and the development team who will build the system against this specification.
Everything described in Sections 1 to 16 is in scope for the working prototype. Section 23 separates the forward-looking product vision from what will actually be demonstrated, so that no capability is over-claimed.
1.2  Scope of the Prototype
SIF Sentinel AI ingests historical and newly reported safety records — Unsafe Acts (UA), Unsafe Conditions (UC), Near Misses and Incidents — and converts each free-text narrative into structured, scored, explainable safety intelligence. The prototype demonstrates the complete journey of a single report:
A raw narrative is ingested and cleaned.
The AI engine predicts its Serious Injury & Fatality (SIF) potential and assigns a risk band.
Hazards, equipment, activity and unsafe conditions are extracted as structured entities.
The report is mapped to the Life Saving Rules it violates.
Semantically similar fatal incidents are retrieved from a 14,914-record fatality knowledge base.
A Fatality Twin projects the escalation path the near miss could have followed.
Corrective and preventive actions, plus a toolbox talk, are generated automatically.
Every result rolls up into site, area, activity and department-level dashboards that leadership can act on.

2.  Problem Statement and Motivation
2.1  The Operational Problem
Oil India Limited receives thousands of UA, UC, Near Miss and Incident reports every month. These are reviewed manually and at long intervals. By the time a pattern is visible in a quarterly review, the conditions that produced it have often already escalated.
The deeper problem is not volume — it is that the most valuable information in a safety report is locked inside free text. A narrative such as "worker contacted energized cable while replacing junction box" contains a hazard, an activity, a barrier failure, a violated Life Saving Rule and a fatality precursor. A conventional reporting system stores it as a row. It does not read it.
2.2  What Existing Systems Cannot Answer
[TABLE START]
Question leadership actually asks | Why current systems fail
Which of our sites is becoming more dangerous? | Counts are stored, but severity potential is never normalised across sites, so a site with many minor reports looks worse than one with three near-fatal events.
Which activities are most likely to lead to a fatality? | Activity is buried in narrative text and is rarely a structured field.
Which Life Saving Rules are violated most often? | Rule mapping is manual, inconsistent between reviewers, and often skipped entirely.
What hidden patterns exist across locations? | Cross-site correlation requires reading every report — nobody has the time.
Which safety barriers keep failing? | Barrier failure is implied by the narrative but never captured as data.
Has this nearly killed someone before? | There is no way to search 15,000 historical fatalities by meaning rather than keyword.
[TABLE END]

3.  Prototype Objectives and Success Criteria
3.1  Guiding Principle
This prototype demonstrates intelligence, not infrastructure. Rather than building a partial version of every enterprise feature, it builds a complete version of the reasoning layer — the part that is genuinely hard, genuinely novel, and genuinely difficult to fake in a demonstration.
3.2  Objectives
O1 — Understand narratives. Extract structured meaning (hazard, equipment, activity, unsafe condition, barrier failure) from unstructured incident text.
O2 — Predict severity potential. Assign a calibrated SIF probability and a four-level risk band to every report.
O3 — Explain every prediction. Never output a score without the evidence that produced it. Trust is the deliverable, not the number.
O4 — Learn from the dead. Retrieve semantically similar fatal cases so that an officer reviewing a near miss can see what it became elsewhere.
O5 — Aggregate into decisions. Roll individual predictions up into site, area, activity, department and rule-level intelligence.
O6 — Forecast, not just report. Project where risk is trending over the coming week.
O7 — Make it conversational. Let an HSE officer interrogate the entire corpus in natural language.
O8 — Close the loop. Generate the CAPA, the toolbox talk and the safety bulletin so insight becomes action.

4.  Overall Description
4.1  Product Perspective
SIF Sentinel AI is a self-contained web application. It sits logically above an organisation's existing incident reporting system: reports flow in (by bulk import or direct entry), intelligence flows out (through dashboards, an API and generated documents). It does not replace the system of record — it becomes the system of insight.

4.3  Operating Environment
Client: Any modern evergreen browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari) at 1366 × 768 or above; responsive down to tablet width.
Server: Linux container running Python 3.11, served by Uvicorn behind Nginx; deployable to any Docker host.
Database: MongoDB 7.x with Atlas Vector Search enabled (local fallback: MongoDB Community with a FAISS side-index).
Network: HTTPS only; outbound access required to the LLM inference endpoint.
4.4  Design and Implementation Constraints
A single database technology (MongoDB) is used for documents, aggregates and vectors — chosen deliberately to keep the prototype stack small enough to build and explain within the hackathon window.
All AI outputs must be traceable to source records; no answer may be produced that cannot cite the reports it came from.
Model inference must run on CPU-only hardware so the demonstration does not depend on GPU availability at the venue.
The system must run fully offline apart from LLM calls, and must degrade gracefully — losing the Copilot must never break the dashboards.
4.5  Assumptions and Dependencies
OSHA incident and fatality narratives are a valid proxy for OIL's internal report format; field mapping is handled at ingestion.
Site, area and department attributes are either present in the data or synthesised consistently for the demonstration corpus, and this is disclosed on screen.
An LLM inference endpoint is reachable during the demonstration; a cached-response fallback is pre-seeded for the Copilot in case of venue network failure.

5.2  Why Two Datasets Matter
Most safety analytics tools train on one corpus and treat severity as a column. Using two corpora changes what the system can do. The incident set teaches the model what a report looks like across the full severity spectrum. The fatality set gives the model a reference library of outcomes — so when a near miss arrives, the system does not merely score it, it can point to the fourteen times that exact combination of conditions actually killed someone.
[TABLE START]
This is the difference judges should noticeA conventional classifier says: this report is high risk.SIF Sentinel AI says: this report is high risk, here are the four phrases that drove that, here are the fourteen fatal cases it resembles at 93% similarity, here is the barrier that failed in all of them, and here is the toolbox talk to run on this shift.
[TABLE END]

5.3  Data Preparation Pipeline
[TABLE START]
Stage | Operation | Output
1. Acquisition | Load raw CSV/JSON exports; preserve original record identifiers | Raw staging collection
2. Cleaning | Strip control characters, normalise casing and whitespace, expand safety abbreviations (LOTO, PTW, PPE, TBSA) | Normalised narratives
3. De-duplication | Near-duplicate detection via MinHash + cosine similarity threshold 0.95 | Unique record set
4. Enrichment | Derive site, area, department, activity and date fields; attach ingestion metadata | Structured document
5. Labelling | Map outcome/severity fields to SIF-positive and SIF-negative classes; weak-label ambiguous cases via rule heuristics | Training set
6. Splitting | Stratified 70 / 15 / 15 train-validation-test split, grouped by site to prevent leakage | Model-ready splits
7. Embedding | Encode every narrative with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 into 384-dim vectors | Vector index
[TABLE END]
5.4  Class Imbalance Strategy
SIF-positive events are, by their nature, the minority class — which is precisely why naive accuracy is a misleading metric. A model that predicts "not SIF" for everything would score well and be useless. Three measures are applied:
Class weighting: the loss function penalises missed SIF cases more heavily than false alarms, because in safety a false negative costs a life and a false positive costs an inspection.
Threshold tuning on recall: the decision threshold is selected on the validation set to maximise recall at a precision floor, not to maximise accuracy.
Fatality-set augmentation: narratives from the fatality corpus supply additional high-severity training signal that the incident corpus alone cannot provide.
6.  System Architecture
6.1  Architectural Overview
The system follows a five-layer, left-to-right pipeline architecture. Data enters at the left, is progressively enriched as it moves right, and is served to the user at the far right. Each layer is independently replaceable: the AI engine can swap models without the dashboard changing, and the dashboard can be rebuilt without touching the data layer.
The architecture is deliberately linear because safety intelligence is a refinement problem, not a routing problem — every report follows the same path from raw text to actionable insight. The full-page diagram overleaf shows the complete flow.

6.5  Design Decisions and Their Rationale
[TABLE START]
Decision | Alternative Considered | Why We Chose It
MongoDB as the single datastore | PostgreSQL + pgvector + separate document store | Narratives, model outputs and vectors all live in one document per report. One database, one query path, zero join complexity, and Atlas Vector Search removes the need for a separate vector service.
FastAPI over Django/Flask | Django REST Framework | Native async handling of model inference, automatic OpenAPI documentation, and Pydantic validation that mirrors our document schema exactly.
Fine-tuned encoder over a pure LLM classifier | Prompting an LLM for every score | Deterministic, reproducible, auditable, sub-100 ms on CPU, and free at inference. A safety score must be defensible; an LLM temperature is not.
RAG over fine-tuned generation | Fine-tuning a generative model on safety text | Every Copilot answer must cite real report IDs. Retrieval makes grounding structural rather than aspirational, and the knowledge base can be updated without retraining.
Pre-computed dashboard aggregates | Live aggregation on every request | Sub-400 ms dashboard interaction is a demo-critical property. Aggregations are refreshed on write and on schedule.
[TABLE END]

10.  AI Intelligence Engine
This section specifies the models themselves. It exists because the engine is the product — a judge should be able to read this section alone and know exactly what is being claimed and how it is being measured.
10.1  Model Inventory
[TABLE START]
Component | Approach | Input → Output | Evaluation Metric
SIF Classifier | DistilBERT fine-tuned, blended with XGBoost over structured features; probability calibrated with isotonic regression | Narrative + metadata → P(SIF) ∈ [0,1] | Recall on SIF class, ROC-AUC, Brier score for calibration
LSR Mapper | Multi-label DistilBERT head over nine rule classes with per-label thresholds | Narrative → set of (rule, confidence) | Micro-F1 and per-rule precision/recall
Hazard & Activity NER | spaCy pipeline augmented with a curated safety gazetteer and LLM back-fill for unseen phrasing | Narrative → typed entity spans | Entity-level F1 against a hand-annotated sample
Risk Band Fusion | Deterministic rule layer combining SIF probability, LSR severity weight and barrier-failure presence | Scores → LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH/CRITICAL | Confusion matrix against domain-reviewed labels
Explainability | SHAP token attribution, summarised into plain language by an LLM constrained to the attributed tokens | Model internals → human explanation | Faithfulness check: does removing top tokens flip the prediction?
Semantic Retriever | all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings over Atlas Vector Search with metadata pre-filtering | Query → top-k similar reports | Precision@10 under domain review
Fatality Twin | Retrieval of nearest fatal cases, then escalation-chain synthesis constrained to observed outcome sequences | Near miss → projected escalation path | Plausibility review; every chain node must trace to a real case
Forecaster | Prophet per hazard category with weekly seasonality and changepoint detection | Historical counts → 7-day projection | MAPE on a held-out trailing window
[TABLE END]

10.2  Risk Band Fusion Logic
The four-level band is not simply a threshold on the SIF probability. Bands are produced by an explicit, auditable rule layer, because a safety classification must be explainable to a regulator, not only to a data scientist.
[TABLE START]
score = 0.55 * P(SIF)      + 0.25 * max(LSR_severity_weight)      + 0.10 * barrier_failure_present      + 0.10 * fatality_similarity_max CRITICAL  if score >= 0.85  OR  (P(SIF) >= 0.90 and barrier_failure_present)HIGH      if score >= 0.65MEDIUM    if score >= 0.40LOW       otherwise # Escalation override: any report matching a fatal case at >= 0.90# similarity is floored at HIGH regardless of computed score.
[TABLE END]

The escalation override matters. It encodes a domain principle directly into the system: if this has already killed someone somewhere, it does not get to be called low risk here.

10.4  Safety Knowledge Graph
Every analysed report contributes nodes and edges to a graph linking sites, activities, hazards, barrier failures, root causes and outcomes. Centrality analysis over this graph surfaces the barriers that fail most often across otherwise unrelated incidents — a pattern that is invisible in tabular reporting.

12.  Flagship AI Features
These eight capabilities are the reason this is a safety intelligence platform rather than a reporting dashboard. Each one is fully implemented in the prototype and each one is demonstrable live.
12.1  Fatality Twin — “What could have happened?”
The single most under-used asset in industrial safety is the near miss, because a near miss is a fatality where one variable happened to differ. The Fatality Twin makes that counterfactual explicit. For any low-consequence report, the system retrieves the nearest fatal cases and projects the escalation chain the event was on.
[TABLE START]
Worked example from the demonstrationInput:  “Worker slipped on oil-contaminated grating while carrying a valve assembly at height.”Twin:   Slip → loss of grip → fall through unguarded opening → fatal head injury.Evidence: 9 matched fatal cases, mean similarity 0.91. Common barrier failure in all 9 — housekeeping and edge protection.Action:  Housekeeping audit raised as C1, edge-protection verification as P1.
[TABLE END]

12.2  AI Safety Copilot
A conversational interface over the entire corpus. It is not a chatbot bolted onto a dashboard — it can query aggregates, retrieve individual reports, and compose both into a grounded answer. Representative queries handled in the demonstration:
“Which site is most dangerous this month, and why?”
“Why is report INC-2026-04821 rated critical?”
“Generate a CAPA for the confined-space entry incidents at Refinery Block B.”
“Summarise today's incidents for the morning HSE meeting.”
“What barrier keeps failing across our electrical maintenance work?”
“Compare energy-isolation violations between Rig-07 and Pipeline Sector 4.”

12.3  Explainable Risk Scoring
Every score carries its evidence. Token-level SHAP attribution identifies which phrases drove the prediction; the rule layer states which structural conditions escalated the band; and both are summarised in a sentence an officer can repeat to a crew. A safety system that cannot justify itself will not be trusted, and an untrusted system will not be used.
12.4  Similar Fatal Incident Retrieval
Semantic search across 14,914 fatality records. Because retrieval is embedding-based rather than keyword-based, a report describing “pressure released while opening line” correctly surfaces fatalities recorded as “stored energy release during depressurisation” — no shared vocabulary, identical physics.
12.5  Smart Recommendations and Toolbox Talk Generation
CAPA generation is precedent-driven: the system reads what actually prevented recurrence in similar historical cases rather than inventing generic advice. Output is structured into immediate corrective actions, systemic preventive actions, training requirements and a five-minute toolbox talk ready to be delivered at the next shift handover.
12.6  Safety Knowledge Graph
Relationships across site, activity, hazard, barrier failure, root cause and outcome are materialised as a navigable graph. Betweenness centrality over the barrier nodes answers a question no dashboard can: which single control, if fixed, would break the most incident pathways?
12.7  Predictive Trend Analysis
Per-hazard seven-day forecasting with changepoint detection. The value is not the number but the direction: knowing that hot-work risk is trending up 18% before the shutdown window begins changes how the week is planned.
12.8  Automated Safety Bulletins
Daily bulletins, weekly rollups and executive summaries generated as formatted PDFs. This closes the loop from detection to distribution — the intelligence reaches the people who never open a dashboard.

14.  User Interface and Visual Design
14.1  Design Principles
Risk is legible in one second. Colour, position and typographic weight all encode severity consistently, so the eye lands on the critical item before the user has finished reading the page.
Never a number without a reason. Every score, band and ranking is one click away from its evidence.
Calm under pressure. This tool is used after something went wrong. The interface stays quiet: generous whitespace, restrained motion, no alarm fatigue from over-use of red.
Colour is never the only signal. Every risk colour is paired with a text label, satisfying WCAG 2.1 AA and colour-vision deficiency requirements.
Density where experts need it. The executive view is sparse; the incident deep-analysis view is dense. The same design system serves both.

17.  Security, Privacy and Governance
17.1  Access Control
A simplified four-role model is implemented: Officer, Manager, Leadership and Administrator. Roles gate both API routes and dashboard navigation. Full enterprise identity integration is out of prototype scope but the role boundary is real, not cosmetic.
17.2  Data Protection
All traffic over HTTPS; no credentials or API keys ever reach the client bundle.
Worker names and personally identifying details are redacted at ingestion — the models operate on circumstance, not identity.
LLM calls transmit narrative text only, with site and personnel identifiers stripped before the request leaves the service layer.
Source documents are immutable; every analysis is an additive, versioned artefact.

24.  Conclusion
SIF Sentinel AI is built on a narrow, defensible thesis: fatalities are preceded by near misses that carried the same conditions and were read as routine. The prototype demonstrates that those conditions are extractable from free text, scorable, explainable, and comparable against a corpus of outcomes that already happened.
The architectural choices reflect that focus. A single database rather than four. Deterministic classifiers where a score must be defensible, retrieval-grounded generation where language is required, and an explicit rule layer where domain knowledge must be encoded rather than learned. Nothing in the stack exists to make the diagram look impressive.
What the prototype delivers is a complete reasoning path from an unstructured sentence typed by a field officer to a prioritised action assigned to a shift — with the evidence for every step visible on screen. That path is the product. Everything in Section 23 is an extension of it.
