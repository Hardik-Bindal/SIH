1. PROBLEM DOMAIN: INDUSTRIAL SAFETY
==================================================

Key terms:
  UA (Unsafe Act)       — Person deviates from safe procedure
  UC (Unsafe Condition) — Physical/environmental hazard exists
  Near Miss             — No injury but COULD have caused one
  Incident              — Actually caused injury/damage
  SIF                   — Serious Injury or Fatality
  SIF Precursor         — Near miss that COULD have been a SIF

CRITICAL DISTINCTION:
  "Actual severity" = what happened to the person
  "SIF potential"   = what COULD have happened under different luck

Example: Wrench falls 1 foot from worker's head. Actual severity: ZERO.
SIF potential: EXTREME (could have killed them).

Other terms:
  Barrier/Control — measure preventing hazard from reaching worker
  Barrier Failure — control was absent, bypassed, removed, defective
  CAPA — Corrective And Preventive Action
  HSE  — Health, Safety, Environment department
  LOTO — Lockout/Tagout procedure
  IOGP — International Association of Oil & Gas Producers

Why traditional review fails: manual, sampled, periodic, isolated,
backward-looking, memory-less.

3. OUR COMPLETE SOLUTION
==================================================

Pipeline for one report:

  Report filed
  → NER extracts hazards, equipment, barrier failures
  → SIF classifier: TF-IDF+LR (text) blended with XGBoost (structured)
  → LSR mapper: multi-label classification to 9 Life Saving Rules
  → Token attribution explains WHY
  → FAISS searches 16,249 records for similar cases
  → Fatality Twin builds escalation chain from matched fatalities
  → Risk fusion: 0.55·P(SIF) + 0.25·LSR + 0.10·barrier + 0.10·similarity
  → Escalation override: fatal match ≥0.90 → floor at HIGH
  → CAPA generated from LSR-keyed templates + cited precedent
  → Full JSON response returned (<2s)

Example walkthrough:
  INPUT: "Guard removed from milling machine. Employee's glove caught in
  rotating spindle. Amputation of three fingers."

  SIF probability: 0.91 | Band: CRITICAL
  LSR: BYPASSING_CONTROLS (0.94), LINE_OF_FIRE (0.78)
  Explanation: "amputation, caught, guard removed"
  Similar fatalities: 8 matches | Fatality Twin: Caught→Barrier fail→Fatality
  CAPA: "Reinstate all removed/bypassed guards before equipment restart."

==================================================
6. DATA PREPROCESSING
==================================================

ETL pipeline:
  1. Text cleaning — whitespace normalisation, abbreviation expansion
  2. Deduplication — exact-text + TF-IDF cosine ≥0.95 (incidents only)
  3. Label creation — SIF from Degree of Injury; LSR from weak supervision
  4. Split — 70/15/15, stratified, grouped by site

Why group by site: prevents data leakage. Model must generalise to unseen
sites. [LIMITATION] Sites are synthetic, so less rigorous than real grouping.


==================================================
7. SIF CLASSIFICATION
==================================================

Binary classification: SIF-potential (1) vs non-SIF-potential (0).

Ensemble: TF-IDF+LR (text, weight 0.65) + XGBoost (structured, weight 0.35)
→ isotonic calibration → threshold tuning (recall-first)

Actual metrics (ml/artifacts/sif_classifier_metrics.json):
  Recall:    0.988 (PPT rounds to 0.99)
  Precision: 0.889 (PPT says 0.89)
  ROC-AUC:   0.969 (PPT rounds to 0.97)
  F1:        0.936
  Threshold: 0.05
  Train/Val/Test: 3,241 / 538 / 1,034

Why recall > precision: Missing a SIF precursor (false negative) can cost
a life. A false alarm (false positive) costs an inspection. We tune for
catching everything.

Smoke detector analogy: beeping while cooking = annoying but safe.
Not beeping during fire = deadly.

8. DISTILBERT
==================================================

[PPT CLAIM] DistilBERT in tech stack.
[ACTUAL] TF-IDF + Logistic Regression (HuggingFace was unreachable).

What to know for judges:

  DistilBERT — 40% smaller, 60% faster, 97% accuracy retained
  Why specified — CPU-friendly, contextual understanding > bag-of-words

Why TF-IDF works here: safety reports use constrained vocabulary where
specific words ("amputation", "fell from height") ARE the signal.

HONEST ANSWER: "SRS specifies DistilBERT. Prototype uses TF-IDF+LR as

==================================================
9. XGBOOST
==================================================

Gradient boosted decision trees. Builds 300 small trees sequentially,
each correcting errors of the previous.

Processes STRUCTURED features: area, department, activity, barrier_failure.
Captures non-linear interactions that text alone cannot.

Why BOTH models: text reads WHAT happened; XGBoost reads WHERE and
WHAT TYPE of work. "Slipped" is ambiguous alone — with area=RIG +
barrier_failure=true, it becomes high-SIF.

Blend weights (0.65/0.35) are manually chosen, not learned.


==================================================
10. LIFE-SAVING RULE CLASSIFICATION
==================================================

9 IOGP Life Saving Rules:
  1. Energy Isolation    2. Work at Height      3. Confined Space
  4. Line of Fire        5. Safe Mechanical Lifting  6. Hot Work
  7. Work Authorisation  8. Bypassing Controls  9. Driving Safety

Multi-LABEL classification (one report can violate multiple rules).
Method: One-vs-Rest Logistic Regression over TF-IDF.

[ACTUAL METRICS] Micro F1 vs weak labels: 0.77 (target 0.85 NOT MET)
Best: Line of Fire (0.91), Work at Height (0.90)
Worst: Work Authorisation (0.07), Bypassing Controls (0.27)

Labels are from WEAK SUPERVISION, not human annotation.


==================================================
11. WEAK SUPERVISION
==================================================

No public ground-truth LSR dataset exists. We create labels via:
  1. Regex patterns on narrative ("scaffold" → WORK_AT_HEIGHT)
  2. Real OSHA structured fields (Event Type = "Fall from elevation" → WORK_AT_HEIGHT)

Labels are noisy by design. Reported F1 is agreement with heuristic,
NOT with human experts. Disclosed in metrics JSON and on screen.


==================================================
12. BARRIER FAILURE EXTRACTION
==================================================

Conservative regex patterns detect when text mentions a failed control:
  "guard had been removed" → barrier_failure = TRUE
  "lockout was not verified" → barrier_failure = TRUE

CRITICAL: barrier_failure = FALSE means "not mentioned", NOT "didn't happen."
Only ~1% of OSHA narratives state a barrier failure (128/16,249).

False positives are worse than misses here — a false barrier flag inflates
risk on invented evidence. Patterns stay conservative and precise.


==================================================
13. SAFETY MEMORY / PRECEDENT RETRIEVAL
==================================================

Answers three questions:
  1. Has this happened before? → top-k similar cases from FAISS
  2. What was the common cause? → majority LSR across matches
  3. What should be done? → CAPA keyed to dominant rule

Process: narrative → 384-dim vector (TF-IDF+SVD) → FAISS search →
retrieve similar incidents and fatalities → analyse common patterns.

"No citation, no answer" — enforced IN CODE. If no match exists, the
system says so explicitly rather than fabricating one.

==================================================
16. RISK FUSION
==================================================

  Risk = 0.55 × P(SIF) + 0.25 × max_LSR_severity + 0.10 × barrier + 0.10 × sim

  P(SIF)            — calibrated classifier output (0-1)
  max_LSR_severity  — max(confidence × rule_severity_weight) across tags
  barrier           — 1.0 if detected, 0.0 otherwise
  sim               — max cosine similarity to any confirmed fatality

  Bands: CRITICAL ≥0.85 | HIGH ≥0.65 | MEDIUM ≥0.40 | LOW <0.40
  Also: P(SIF) ≥0.90 AND barrier_failure → CRITICAL regardless

  Weights are MANUALLY CHOSEN based on domain priority, not learned.
  Documented and auditable. Could be calibrated on OIL outcome data.


==================================================
17. ESCALATION OVERRIDE
==================================================

IF fatality_similarity ≥ 0.90 AND current_band < HIGH → override to HIGH.

Why: a report 90%+ similar to a confirmed fatality must NEVER be scored
low risk. This is a HARD domain rule that no amount of low model scores
can override. "If it already killed someone, it cannot be low risk."


==================================================
18. CALIBRATION
==================================================

Isotonic regression maps raw probabilities to calibrated ones.
After calibration: P(SIF)=0.80 means ~80% of such reports are truly SIF.
Brier score: 0.052 (lower = better calibrated).

Threshold selection: sweep 0.05-0.95, pick threshold with highest recall
where precision ≥ 0.60. Result: threshold = 0.05.


==================================================
19. CLASS IMBALANCE
==================================================

SIF events are rare. Mitigations:
  1. class_weight="balanced" (LR)
  2. scale_pos_weight (XGBoost)
  3. Recall-first threshold = 0.05
  4. Isotonic calibration

Accuracy is MISLEADING — a model always predicting "non-SIF" gets 95%
accuracy but catches zero dangers. Use recall, precision, ROC-AUC instead.
